# Ghost Boat quick reference

One page to keep beside the console. This card is on the console too: press `?`, and `h` in it for the full [captain's manual](manual.md) (or `F1` straight to it).

## First minutes

1. Power up the trainer, choose **Training** on the main menu, and run **Civilian 1: The Console**. The eleven civilian tutorials take about two hours and teach one idea each; then **Naval 1: Taking Command** teaches the navy's orders. The 34 lessons run in four tracks: Civilian 1 to 11, Naval 1 to 7, Ghost 1 to 12 and Hunter 1 to 4. Ghost 1 to 7 teach the attack boat's weapons: silent running, the solution, straight runners, homing torpedoes, the line-up, jamming and the beam. Ghost 8 to 12 teach mines, pods and people aboard, base and orders, the patrol and the trip home. Hunter 1 to 4 teach the ping, the guns, the escort and the flight deck.
2. `Space` pauses at any time. `?` opens this card and pauses the trainer; `h` in it opens the manual, `q` goes back. `/` searches the card or the manual: `Enter` finds a word, `n` and `N` step through the matches.
3. `q` leaves an exercise and saves it, after asking (`q` again to leave; Options turns the question off); **Continue** on the main menu picks it up again. `Esc` never quits, and never leaves a fight, the base or the patrol chart.
4. On the title screen, `↑`/`↓` choose, `Enter` opens, and `Esc` or `q` goes back to the main menu; `q` there quits. Scenarios holds the challenges, War Campaign the war, and Options the theme, colours, sign-in and Wild Spaceland (off by default). On a list of exercises, `d` sets the difficulty, `i` iron mode, `l` the loadout, `b` the boat, `w` the war, `n` the vessel's name (`r` draws one) and `o` the bare conn. Shift steps a choice back: `D`, `I`, `L`, `B`, `W`.

## Signing in

Any key skips the sign-in. `C` on the title screen (or Options, then Captain and sign-in) opens the captains page: `Enter` signs in, `a` always logs in as this captain, `n` adds a captain, `r` renames one, `c` changes career, `f` files Form FP-1138, `s` turns the sequence on or off. `--no-signin` skips sign-in for one run.

## Flying: autopilot or orders

**A civilian hull flies on autopilot.** The arrows act at once.

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| `←` `→` | Steer, 5° a press (`Shift` for 1°) |
| `Shift+↑` `Shift+↓` | Pitch, 5° a press |
| `↑` `↓` | The speed the autopilot holds (or the throttle, flying by hand) |
| `C` | Autopilot on, or manual control (civilian hulls; the navy has none). A Shift, because it radiates |
| `x` | Cut the drive; on autopilot, come to rest |

**A warship flies by orders.** The arrows **dial** an order; nothing happens until you press `Enter`, and the crew takes a few seconds to answer. With a map in focus (a plot, or the patrol chart in a fight), the plain arrows move the map instead, as on the patrol screen; `c` brings it home.

| Key | Order |
|---|---|
| `←` `→` | Dial a heading, 5° a step (`Shift` for 1°) |
| `Shift+↑` `Shift+↓` | Dial a pitch, 5° a step |
| `↑` `↓` | Dial the engine telegraph: all stop, ahead one third, two thirds, standard, full, flank |
| `Enter` | Give the dialled order (`Esc` drops it) |
| `x` | All stop, without `Enter` |

**Newton, not the sea.** Turning the nose does not change where you are going; only burning does. To slow down, the boat must turn round and burn (`full stop` on the conn does both).

## Reading the contacts table

| Column | Means |
|---|---|
| `S1`, `S2` | Contacts, by the order you heard them |
| `TYPE` | The crew's best guess: `unknown`, `civilian?`, `military?`, then a class. Only an opinion. `info s1` shows the odds |
| `RANGE km` | A range and its error. `?200-900` is a limit from the signal and the crew's calls, never a solution |
| `TMA` | How good the solution is: `POOR`, `FAIR`, `GOOD`. You need `FAIR` or better to shoot |
| `SIG` | dB above your threshold. A change in it is not a range: closing and getting louder look the same, and so do opening and going quiet. Only a solution gives range |
| Rose `HEARD AT ~` | How far off you could be heard now, by your own estimate. A red-shaded contact is inside it |
| Course | Click the focused plan to arm one; `Enter` gives it, `Esc` drops it, and it lapses after 8 s |
| Overlay | `p` takes up the pencil: click a note, drag a line, click a mark to rub out. Pencil down: drag a note to move it. `R` takes up the ruler, `G` the next sheet |
| Plot marks | `⊗ launch 12m` is where you were loud: a ring grows at your speed. It never says whether anyone heard |
| Plot | Drag to pan; `centre s1` follows a contact, `c` comes home at the standard range |
| `HEAR` | Which sensors have it: `P` passive array (`F` where the fight runs on real field units), `I` infrared, `X` beacon (it is announcing itself), `x` beacon gone silent, `E` EM (it is transmitting), `M` magnetic (it is close) |
| `DZ km` | Height above (`+`) or below (`-`) you, once range is known. Ranges are 3-D |

Symbols: `○` friendly, `◇` hostile, `□` neutral, `✣` unknown. A fighter wears the top half: `◠ ⋀ ⊓ ⌒`. `⊕ WPT` is a waypoint from your orders, not a contact. In a career fight, `⌂` is a berth from command's chart, and `EDGE` on the rose is the way out of a gravity well, with the range to its edge.

## Facts worth knowing

- A ship that burns or pings is heard far beyond where it can hear or see you.
- Range comes from a change of course. On a straight course the solution stays `POOR`.
- Burning makes you deaf as well as loud.
- A homing torpedo chases the loudest thing it hears. A static buoy is a loud lure.
- A carrier sees with her craft. Her own hull is too loud to hear a quiet boat. Pickets listen far out, a screen guards against torpedoes, and each faint ring on the plot is how far out a craft can be and still make the deck. The lesson **Hunter 4: The Flight Deck** teaches it.
- Craft are loud and half deaf. What one hears, the destroyers hear. `Launch transients` in the log mean a flight deck, and craft on the way.
- A jammer blinds a homing seeker, not a straight runner. On a `GOOD` solution the straight runners' fan closes.
- Jamming hides you inside its cone and tells everyone else exactly where you are.
- A civilian hull is heard far off and hears little. A warship is the reverse.

## The career

New Game starts a career: one captain, one boat and one war, in patrols from base. In the black the console shows the patrol chart, north up: the bodies with their wells' edges dashed, the places, our boat, and each contact report (`◇r1`) inside the circle of its error, which grows as it ages. The arrow keys or a drag move the chart, and `c` brings it home, as in a fight. Traffic nobody reported is not drawn. The strip under the chart holds the three clocks: fuel against the bingo line `│` (the fuel home at standard warp plus a 10% reserve; `BINGO` below it), food (water rations included) and crew wear. None is restored in the black.

After each patrol comes the yard: the refit is paid in days, by the jobs chosen. The essentials take 30 and the hull is always mended. A damaged system is repaired, or patched for 2 days and capped at 90% until a later refit. From Commander a quirk fixed (10) and fresh torpedoes (3); from Captain new equipment (15). Standing above +2 cuts every job's days, up to a quarter. A hull past about 180 days' work pays the boat off.

Each war keeps a diary, by patrol, with a war patrol report for each patrol: **War diary** at base, or `diary` at base or on the chart. When the war ends it is written to `diaries/` beside the saves, as plain text and Markdown, and **War diaries** on the main menu reads it again.

An order missed in the black is learned at the next port. Drift Station is a port: fuel and a briefing, no food or rest. People aboard beyond the crew eat: the column gives the days of food they cost.

### In a career fight

| Order | Does |
|---|---|
| `warp out` | Leave the fight for the chart: at rest (`full stop` first, a loud burn), clear of every well's edge, drive cold, for the whole spin-up (`warp_spinup_s`, 15 minutes by default, WARP on the top bar). No contact stops it. While it spins up you radiate as hard as your drive at full power, magnetometers feel you hundreds of kilometres off, and your array is deaf |
| `recover` | Bring aboard a lifeboat's or pod's people as survivors, of any side: within 2 km and all but stopped, 10 minutes. How many is unknown until they are aboard. An order to pick up survivors counts them. In a lesson, the same order brings aboard a practice shot adrift by her beacon |
| `land` | Put the operatives ashore at the ordered point: within 5 km and all but stopped, 30 minutes |
| `search s3` | Search a crippled merchant: within 2 km and all but stopped, three hours. Finds her cargo, papers or dispatches, and a passenger who matters, brought off as a prisoner |
| `confirm fire` | Fire on a contact the crew call a pod or lifeboat, within 30 s of their query. A torpedo whose line or spread passes one is queried too. A grave breach: the patrol fails and the captain is relieved |

`setup patrol` shows the patrol chart as it stood at the drop. A career fight ends only when you warp out or the boat is lost; inside a well, the goal "Clear the well." is met once clear of the edge, where warp becomes possible, and meeting it ends nothing. `helm n1` points the bow on the way out, and the rose's `EDGE` tag gives its range; stop well clear, since the pull draws a stopped boat back. While the fight runs, `q` does not leave it. `Q` saves the fight and goes back to the title. Tonnage counts only once the boat is home.

### The crew

Nine named crew, mixed, and the hands. Skill reads Green, Qualified, Steady, Seasoned or Expert; wear Fresh, Tired, Worn or Spent; how well they know the boat none, some, good or full. The base's **Crew** screen shows them (`Space` picks two, `s` trades their watches, `w` the scheme); while command has taken the exec, or offers a newer boat, the base lists the choices. On the pick for a new boat, `Enter` picks as `Space` does, and `Enter` on the last row takes the boat. **Shore leave**: 14 days ashore, slower wear for three patrols.
